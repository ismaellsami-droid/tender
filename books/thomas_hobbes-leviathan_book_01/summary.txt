These writings by Thomas Hobbes explore human nature by starting from the mechanisms of sensory perception and imagination. The author defines thought as a sequence of internal motions triggered by external objects, thereby grounding all knowledge in physical experience. He then analyzes the crucial role of language, which he considers an indispensable tool for transforming thoughts into science and logical reasoning. Hobbes also examines the passions—such as desire and fear—that guide voluntary actions and shape the notions of good and evil. Finally, the text addresses power and honors, laying the psychological foundations necessary for understanding social and political life.
